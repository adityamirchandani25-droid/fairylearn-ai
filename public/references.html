WHAT SHAPED THIS SITE

Sources & project credits.

These sources support the learning modules and document the tools used to build the site. Lesson explanations and examples were written for this project; they are not copied from the linked works.

Learning content

  1. Google for Developers, “What is Machine Learning?” ↗

    Used for the basic explanation of models, training examples, prediction, and generation in Lesson 1.

  2. Google for Developers, “Introduction to Large Language Models” ↗

    Further reading for the generative AI and language model learning paths.

  3. OpenAI Help Center, “Prompt engineering best practices for ChatGPT” ↗

    Used for the clear task, context, and revision technique in Lesson 2.

  4. UNESCO, “Guidance for generative AI in education and research” ↗

    Used for the education, privacy, and human judgment considerations in Lesson 3.

  5. NIST, “AI Risk Management Framework: Generative AI Profile” ↗

    Used for Lesson 3’s emphasis on trustworthy information, bias, and verifying AI outputs.

  6. OpenAI, “Text generation” ↗

    Further reading for the advanced building-with-models learning path.

  7. Technology Student Association, “Themes and Problems: Webmaster” ↗

    Used to match the 2027–2028 high school Webmaster challenge: AI fundamentals, practical tools, ethical use, and progress tracking.

Tools, assets, and AI assistance

  • Frameworks: No website builder, CMS, React, Vue, or other front-end framework is used. The site uses original HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with a Node.js server.
  • Services: Supabase Auth documentation ↗ guided account access; OpenAI Structured Outputs documentation ↗ guided server-side personal lessons.
  • Fonts: DM Sans ↗ and Lora ↗, loaded from Google Fonts with local fallbacks.
  • Logo: The Fairylearn logo was supplied by the project team. The journey illustration and other interface graphics are drawn in CSS and SVG in this project.
  • Generative AI disclosure: OpenAI Codex assisted with planning, writing, and revising code and learning text for this site. When a signed-in learner requests a personal lesson, GPT creates it from their selected learning profile; the site labels that lesson as AI-generated. The team must review the final work, verify factual claims and rights to assets, and describe its actual AI use in the official Resources and AI Reflection form.
